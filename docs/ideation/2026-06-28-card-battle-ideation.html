Card Battle Enjoyment

Ranked directions for making Escape's main card-battle loop more enjoyable, using Slay the Spire as a reference for readable intent, scarce turn choices, and identity-building rewards without cloning its full combat model.

Evidence

Grounding Context

Codebase context. Escape already has simultaneous card combat, speed ordering, poison, burn, stun, items, relics, collection versus combat-hand state, enemy-card stealing, rest-room upgrades/removals, starter kits, boss specials, and a balance simulator. Runtime code is concentrated in src/scenes/Battle.ts, src/game/combat.ts, src/game/cardSelection.ts, src/game/rewards.ts, src/data/cards.ts, and src/data/enemies.ts.

Current friction. Normal enemies are rendered mostly as card backs and their chosen card is picked during the player action flow. Bosses have interval telegraphs, but normal fights hide the information that would make speed, block, stun, and healing decisions feel intentional. The player has one action per round, and cards refresh only after the whole combat hand has been used.

Past repo learnings. Keep Battle as the combat authority. Dungeon room threat can shape pre-battle pressure, but should not become a second combat system. Visible Phaser UI changes need layout discipline and browser smoke because prior screens regressed through overlapping text. Progression should shape runs, not become raw permanent stat creep, and Daily Descents should stay comparable.

External context. Slay the Spire-like feel is less about copying exact nouns and more about a loop where the player can answer: what is the enemy about to do, what scarce resource do I spend this turn, and what deck identity am I reinforcing? Slay the Spire, Into the Breach, Cobalt Core, Monster Train, and Wildfrost all point toward readable threats, timing pressure, and reward choices that reinforce a plan.

1. Intent Strip Plus Speed Timeline

Reveal the normal enemy's committed next action or action family, its speed, and its main effect before the player chooses. Pair that with a clearer speed timeline so a player can see whether Quick Jab lands before a Hex, whether Guard matters this round, and whether stun can prevent an incoming action. This is the most direct Slay-like improvement because it changes the basic turn from guessing into planning while preserving Escape's simultaneous resolver.

Axis
Enemy intent and turn readability
Basis
direct: src/scenes/Battle.ts renders normal enemy cards as backs and picks normal enemy actions from weighted hidden cards; boss specials already have telegraph text. src/game/combat.ts already resolves action order by speed. external: Slay the Spire, Into the Breach, and Cobalt Core show that explicit incoming threats can create difficulty through sequencing instead of hidden information.
Rationale
This turns existing card speed, block, stun, and status effects into readable answers. It is high value because it improves every battle turn and makes later card/enemy design easier to explain.
Downsides
Needs careful battle layout and a clean intent summary format. Revealing too much exact information could reduce tension, so the first version may use action families plus speed rather than full cards.

2. Authored Enemy Scripts

Give normal enemies small recurring behavior packages instead of mostly generic random card pools. A Bandit might pressure speed, a Cultist might rotate into status, and an Armored Goblin might force block-breaking decisions. Keep random variation, but make the pattern legible enough that players learn opponents over a run.

Axis
Enemy archetypes, statuses, and boss patterns
Basis
direct: src/data/enemies.ts already defines named enemies and tiers, while spawnEnemy() mostly fills their cards from generic random depth pools. src/scenes/Battle.ts applies shared weighted card selection. external: Monster Train and Wildfrost show that recognizable opponent patterns can carry combat identity.
Rationale
Players remember enemies when they force different card evaluations. This also compounds future content work: new enemy equals small script plus card package, not a hand-tuned AI rewrite.
Downsides
Requires enemy data authoring and simulator updates. Over-scripted enemies could become solved if scripts are too short or too deterministic.

3. Status Lanes And Counterplay

Make poison, burn, stun, block, and armor visible as lanes with upcoming ticks, expiry, and next-round impact. Then add narrow counterplay verbs over time: burn could pressure block, stun could delay a telegraphed action, and future support cards could cleanse or ward. This deepens the existing status system instead of adding a new combat model.

Axis
Enemy archetypes, statuses, and boss patterns
Basis
direct: src/data/cards.ts already defines poison, burn, and stun; src/game/combat.ts applies status ticks, stun consumption, and status merging; status cards already influence combat scoring. The Battle scene already has status text, but not a future-facing lane view.
Rationale
Status-heavy cards become more satisfying when delayed payoff and danger are visible. This pairs especially well with intent visibility because statuses can become answers to known threats.
Downsides
UI density risk is real on the Phaser battle screen. Counterplay should start small, or the status matrix will outgrow the card pool.

4. Reward Impact Preview

On card rewards, upgrades, removals, and steals, show exactly what changes in the actual combat hand: enters hand now, replaces another card, improves a role, or stays collection-only for now. This is a low-risk clarity win because the code already computes the answer; the player just does not see the consequence clearly.

Axis
Deckbuilding rewards and hand composition
Basis
direct: RunState separates cardCollection from combatHand, and refreshCombatHand() runs after deck changes. src/game/cardSelection.ts scores and selects the hand automatically. Battle rewards currently add enemy cards to collection, which can hide whether the next fight changed.
Rationale
This fixes the question “did that card matter?” without changing balance. It also prepares the UX for deeper reward ideas later.
Downsides
The preview must be accurate and concise. If it exposes too much scoring detail, it could make rewards feel spreadsheet-like.
Confidence
76%
Complexity
Low

5. Enemy Archetype Rewards

Keep the distinctive “steal from the enemy” reward identity, but make certain enemies carry recognizable archetype rewards. Beating a Cultist might offer a status-oriented tool, while beating an Armored Goblin might offer block pressure or anti-block. The reward should feel like the player learned from the fight, not just looted a random stronger card.

Axis
Deckbuilding rewards and hand composition
Basis
direct: Victory already offers enemy-card theft, and starter kits already show that signature card identity can shape runs. src/data/enemies.ts has named enemies that can carry tighter card packages. external: Strong deckbuilders make rewards reinforce a build identity rather than only increase raw efficiency.
Rationale
This preserves Escape's own reward flavor while making deckbuilding more memorable. It also connects enemy authoring and reward design into one content pipeline.
Downsides
Too many guaranteed signatures could reduce reward variety. This depends on authored enemy scripts or at least tighter enemy packages.

6. Boss Rhythm Boards

Turn boss specials from isolated interval messages into a visible repeating rhythm board, such as setup, pressure, special, recovery. The first version should focus on readability and pattern mastery, not full interrupt mechanics. Later, status counterplay could hook into the rhythm once the board itself is proven fun.

Axis
Enemy archetypes, statuses, and boss patterns
Basis
direct: Bosses already define named specials with interval, speed, and effects in src/data/enemies.ts; BattleScene already shows telegraph text on special rounds. Boss fights are mandatory, so their readability matters more than optional normal fights.
Rationale
Bosses become tests of learning and preparation rather than larger HP pools with occasional spikes. This is the cleanest place to make readable pattern combat feel dramatic.
Downsides
The verifier correctly flagged interrupt/soften windows as weakly grounded. Keep those as future variants, not part of the initial idea.

7. Prepared Answer Slot

Let the player hold or ready one card for an upcoming visible threat. This is intentionally narrower than a full energy or draw/discard system: one prepared answer, one clear future problem, and the existing one-action cadence stays intact. It should only be explored after enemy intent is visible, because otherwise there is no meaningful future state to prepare against.

Axis
Player resource cadence and action economy
Basis
reasoned: The current loop has one action per round and hand exhaustion; visible intent creates a new need to bank an answer. direct: The existing combatHand and used-card tracking provide card availability, but there is no current retain slot, so this is the least grounded survivor.
Rationale
If the top idea succeeds, the next pressure point becomes access timing: the player can see a threat but may not be able to line up the right card. This adds foresight without a full tempo rewrite.
Downsides
Verifier marked this weak because no hold/react seam exists today. It should be brainstormed only as a follow-up to visible intent, not as the first implementation.

Critique

Rejection Summary

# Idea Reason Rejected
1 Tempo Budget Per Turn Refuted by verifier: too much combat-system rewrite relative to the current one-action round contract.
2 Pocket Items Refuted by verifier: breaks item-as-action economy without enough grounding.
3 Enemy Signature Card Capture as standalone Too close to existing enemy-card reward behavior; folded into Enemy Archetype Rewards.
4 Earned Second Beat / Mid-Cycle Refresh Interesting, but pushes against one-action cadence; keep as a possible brainstorm variant after intent and cadence are clearer.
5 Stance Cards Weak basis today; no existing stance-like seam in cards, statuses, relics, or resolver.
6 Full boss interrupt/soften/exploit mechanics Countdown and rhythm board are grounded, but interrupt windows need a later brainstorm once status counterplay is chosen.
7 Role-Gap Reward Offers as full role taxonomy Useful but invented too much structure for the current code; downgraded into Reward Impact Preview plus Enemy Archetype Rewards.
8 Defensive Cards That Auto-Catch Threats Duplicate of Prepared Answer Slot with more automation and weaker grounding.

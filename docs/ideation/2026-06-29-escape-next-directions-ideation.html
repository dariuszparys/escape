Grounding Context

Escape is a Phaser 3 + TypeScript + Vite browser roguelite: a top-down Zelda-style dungeon of 10 rooms where every fight is resolved as simultaneous-choice card combat. Run-local Gold and persistent Ember meta-currency; all art is generated at boot from pixel string-maps and all SFX are synthesized at runtime — zero external assets. The codebase is architecturally clean (pure, tested game logic under src/game + src/dungeon; Phaser scenes only sync and render) but content-thin, and its most distinctive mechanic — the double-blind card duel — is the genre's sparsest, least-explored territory.

Codebase Context

  • Combat (combat.ts resolveRound): both sides reveal one action, sorted by speed (player wins ties); block is per-round only; one card per round, no energy/mana. Hardcoded 1v1.
  • Hand (cardSelection.ts): the 5-card hand is auto-composed by a hidden scorer that forces ≥3 offensive — the player picks which card to play, but not which cards form the hand.
  • Planning board (just shipped): previews enemy intent, speed order, and status before you commit.
  • Meta: Embers from depth milestones (max 6/run); Campfire shop; chronicle.ts stores the last 10 runs; daily.ts is a deterministic seeded run.

Verified gaps & signals

  • Strong enemies (knight/necromancer/ogre) have no combat script — they play randomly, exactly when stakes peak.
  • cardUpgrade.ts upgrades only damage/block/heal; status upgrades are a paid no-op that still rename the card with +.
  • evaluateRoomThreatEscape returns grantBattleReward:false in every branch — the flee reward is dead code.
  • combatFeedback.ts exists as a 64-byte stub; the enemy never hurt-flashes.
  • The CardEffect union is closed; applyAction is a hardcoded if/else, so new verbs require resolver edits.
  • Genre: 850+ deckbuilders by 2024 — crowded on features, under-explored on feel and the simultaneous/Yomi niche.

I1

Make Reading the Enemy the Core Skill

Combat depth Confidence: High Complexity: Medium
Description

The planning board you just shipped is a passive readout — and against the hardest fights it is pointed at a random number generator. Turn the double-blind duel into a genuine mind-game by layering four moves on top of the board: (1) script the strong enemies so the board has something real to predict; (2) give action families asymmetric prediction payoffs (a correctly-timed block earns a counter, a read-and-punish does bonus damage) so reading the opponent pays; (3) let select enemies bluff — show a false intent summary with a learnable tell; (4) optionally let players spend gold to buy down uncertainty for one round. Each layer ships independently and compounds.

Basis
directStrong enemies have no combatScript (enemies.ts: knight/necromancer/ogre, tier strong), so planEnemyIntent falls through to a weighted-random pickFallbackCard — verified. EnemyIntentRevealMode = 'summary' | 'exact' and .summary is a separate struct field from the resolved .action, so a false summary is renderable without touching resolveRound (it needs a new summary-override path, not just the existing flag).
externalSirlin's Yomi design canon: asymmetric payoffs (attack→combo, block→draw, dodge→counter) that are "hard to compute" build intuition, not lookup tables. Inscryption's rule-bending bosses and the sparse Rogue Paper Scissors/Handmancers subgenre show the territory is wide open.
Rationale

This is the single biggest cross-frame convergence: the difficulty curve is literally inverted — fights get statistically harder but mechanically dumber, and the readability feature goes dark exactly when stakes peak. EnemyCombatScript.pattern is cheap to extend, so this retroactively justifies the board the team just built and stakes out Escape's most defensible differentiator.

Downsides

Asymmetric payoffs are the hardest thing in the game to balance — the raison d'être of the balance harness in I4. Bluffing can feel arbitrary without a clear, consistent tell. Scope is large if attempted at once; treat the four layers as a sequence, not one ship.

Combines with → I4 (the balance harness makes payoff tuning safe), I5 (a Nemesis that biases its script against your habits is the boss form of this), I6 (reads matter more once you control your own hand).

I2

Escape Becomes a Verb: Push-Your-Luck Banking

Run economy Confidence: High Complexity: Medium
Description

The game is named Escape, but leaving is never a strategy — death just resets you to zero, and Gold has only two mid-run uses. Make the title a mechanic: at each door you may bank your run's Gold into a guaranteed Ember payout, or push deeper for richer loot you forfeit entirely if you fall. Unbanked value grows with depth; capture costs you only what you didn't bank. Fix the reward function so the deeper run genuinely pays more (today loot decays toward unusable Gold). Optional escalators: a leveraged-debt curse ("The Ledger") and a hunting-boss clock that makes lingering cost distance.

Basis
externalClank! / Can't Stop push-your-luck canon — the threat of losing banked progress is more potent than a death that resets you to zero anyway. It also serves both meta camps the genre splits on: cautious players bank, greedy players push, and persistent Embers stay intact.
directReward entropy is real: in rewards.ts the relic branch falls through to Gold once randomRelic returns null (all 4 owned), the item branch only ever mints potions (randomItemIdForDepth), and iron_armor exists in ITEM_DEFS with zero drop path — so the reward function decays as you progress, punishing your best runs.
Rationale

Three frames independently landed here. It gives Gold genuine weight (a third use: something to lose), converts the forward-only crawl into a tension arc, and makes the title literal — all with no new content, just a decision surface on the doors that already exist.

Downsides

Banking can become a dominant "always bail at room 6" line if the curve isn't tuned — needs the deeper-pays-more fix to hold, and the balance harness to verify. The hunting-boss and debt escalators are separable; don't bundle them into the MVP.

Combines with → I3 (banked outcomes become the shareable daily glyph), I4 (harness models the new skip/bail economy the simulator currently ignores).

I3

The Seeded Engine You Already Paid For

Social meta Confidence: High Complexity: Small
Description

The Daily Descent is fully deterministic from daily-YYYY-MM-DD — everyone faces the byte-identical dungeon and offer sequence — yet it produces zero social value: no sharing, no leaderboard, no comparison. Harvest it. Emit a Wordle-style spoiler-free result glyph (depth + outcome grid + a few key choices) players paste anywhere, and a local "ghost" overlay so you can compare lines against your past attempts or a pasted run — all over copy-paste codes, no backend. Optionally make the Daily a pure-skill mode (no Ember benefits) so two players' runs are genuinely the same.

Basis
directdaily.ts dailySeed returns `daily-${dailyKey(date)}`; DailyRecord holds only date/seed/bestDepth/escaped/attempts — no sharing, ghost, or comparison anywhere. The seeded-run contract (explicit, serializable RNG) is precisely what makes a pasted ghost replay identically.
externalWordle proved a shared spoiler-free daily glyph drives organic discussion; Strava segments show identical-route async comparison ("did you take a better line here?") is a community engine with no live opponent.
Rationale

Four frames converged on this as the cheapest high-leverage move: the expensive engineering (a reproducible deterministic run) is already paid for. It's the single most plausible path to a community for a solo browser dev with no servers.

Downsides

Glyph design must stay genuinely spoiler-free. Ghost replay must be guarded against the seed contract drifting (any new RNG-consuming system risks invalidating old ghosts — see the harness in I4). Virality is never guaranteed.

Combines with → I2 (banked outcomes are richer glyph content), I4 (seed-stability assertions protect ghosts).

I4

The Compounding Content Engine

Content architecture Confidence: Med-High Complexity: High
Description

The content is thin (17 cards, 4 relics, 3 scripted enemies) not because authoring is expensive but because every new mechanic edits a closed resolver. Pay the architecture down once: refactor applyAction into a handler registry + combat event bus (onRoundStart, onDamageDealt, onCardPlayed…). After that, synergy tags, relic "Jokers," conditional cards, and a procedural enemy generator (driven by the existing preference grammar) all become data and subscribers, not resolver edits. Gate it with an extended balance harness that models flee/skip and asserts seed-stability, so generated content ships safely.

Basis
directCardEffect is a closed union (damage|block|heal|status) and applyAction is a hardcoded if/else with no fallthrough — verified; adding a verb does nothing until the resolver is edited. The enemy preference grammar (EnemyCombatPreference = fast_damage|damage|block_damage|status|block|heal, pattern: [][]) already exists as generator fuel. balanceSimulator.ts declares BALANCE_ENCOUNTER_POLICY = 'fight-taken-baseline' with no skip/flee path.
externalBalatro's Jokers and Slay the Spire's relics are the genre's highest build-defining content density per byte — and both are event subscribers in spirit. The grounding's "legible base + deep modifiers" lesson is exactly this shape.
Rationale

This is the leverage keystone under I1, I5, and most future content. It converts "every mechanic costs a resolver edit + a regression pass on the seed contract" into "every mechanic costs a data entry," and the harness is the precondition that makes the generators trustworthy rather than a QA liability.

Downsides

Highest complexity here — an event bus touches the most-tested file in the codebase and risks the seeded-run contract if done carelessly. It pays off only if followed by the content that exploits it; as a standalone refactor it's invisible to players. Sequence it deliberately (bus → synergy/relics → generator).

Combines with → I1 (prediction-payoff verbs need the open union), I5 (Nemesis biasing reuses the generator), I3/I2 (the harness's seed-stability + skip modeling protect both).

I5

The Chronicle Comes Alive: A Nemesis That Learns You

Adaptive meta Confidence: Medium Complexity: Medium
Description

The chronicle records the last 10 runs as inert stats — and notably not what killed you or how you play. Add a couple of fields (the killer's archetype, your dominant action family) and turn dead telemetry into personalized, escalating content: deterministically seed a Nemesis into a future run — the thing that beat you, evolved, biasing its script to counter the family you lean on. Because the core is a double-blind duel, "it has learned your habits" is genuinely threatening. Lighter variants of the same data move: spaced-repetition coaching (re-present the situations you keep failing) and campfire ghosts (past runs as warm, named echoes).

Basis
directchronicle.ts RunChronicleEntry stores runId/completedAt/seed/dailyKey/escaped/depth/enemiesDefeated/gold/emberReward — verified: no killer, no deck snapshot, no playstyle tag, and nothing reads it to adapt. The persistence already exists; only the fields and a consumer are missing.
externalShadow of Mordor's Nemesis system and Inscryption's meta-narrative ("bosses that cheat / remember you") are the canon for turning recorded play into personalized antagonists. Spaced-repetition (SM-2) is the canon for the coaching variant.
Rationale

Three frames reached for the chronicle. It turns an inert stats store into the meta-layer's reason to exist, and it lands in the double-blind niche — a Nemesis that reads you is the boss-scale version of I1. Crucially it's the meta the purist camp endorses: it sharpens, it doesn't grant raw power.

Downsides

"Adaptive AI" risks feeling unfair or like rubber-banding if the bias is too strong or opaque — the grudge must be legible. Nemesis injection must be a pure function of chronicle + seed to honor the seeded-run contract. Authoring "evolved" variants leans on the generator in I4 to avoid bespoke cost.

Combines with → I4 (generator produces the evolved variants), I1 (Nemesis script-bias is asymmetric reads at boss scale), I3 (a defeated-by-Nemesis run is a great glyph).

I6

Give the Player the Hand They Fight With

Combat agency Confidence: High Complexity: Medium
Description

You choose which card to play each round — but you never choose your hand. The 5-card combat hand is auto-composed by a hidden scorer that force-keeps ≥3 offensive cards, silently suppressing defensive or status-focused builds the player might want. The tell that this hurts: the team shipped rewardImpact previews specifically to explain why your new card won't be used. Give composition back along a spectrum, lightest first: (a) expose the scorer as a per-run "doctrine" dial (Aggressive / Bulwark / Affliction) that re-sorts the hand; (b) let players pin/bench cards manually; (c) the bold end — a "singularity" build around a small modifiable spine, so each reward mutates one card instead of diluting a pile.

Basis
directcardSelection.ts: MIN_OFFENSIVE_CARDS = 3 and the score tier*100 + damage*8 + block*4 + heal*4 + status + speed; selectCombatHand sorts the whole collection and slices the top 5 with no player input over composition — verified. rewardImpact.ts enumerates replaces_card / improves_role / collection_only / unchanged, i.e. UI built to warn that a reward is inert.
reasonedIn a simultaneous-choice game the meta-skill is supposed to be deckcraft, but here it's automated away — and a "your loot does nothing" preview is the codebase admitting the auto-hand creates dead rewards. Surfacing the scorer (rather than overriding it) is the lightest change that converts every drop from "the sorter decides" into agency, without rebuilding combat.
Rationale

Four frames flagged the auto-hand as a buried design opinion the player never agreed to. It directly makes the existing reward stream (and I1's reads) matter more, and the doctrine-dial rung is genuinely cheap — it reuses the scorer the game already runs.

Downsides

Manual curation risks re-introducing the per-fight micro the design deliberately avoids — the dial keeps the "no fiddly micro" philosophy while the pin/bench and singularity rungs trade more of it away. Picking the rung is a design-identity decision, not just scope.

Combines with → I1 (a hand you chose makes reads sharper), I4 (synergy tags give curation real decisions), I7 (fixing the status-upgrade lie is part of making curation honest).

I7

Make Every Hit Land: Feel & Honesty Pass

Game feel Confidence: High Complexity: Small
Description

In a market with 850+ deckbuilders crowded on features, feel is the open lane — and Escape's most frequent friction fires on literally every attack: the enemy never flinches. There's no hurt-flash on the enemy sprite, no floating damage/heal numbers, no status-application ping, no screen punch on big hits, and combatFeedback.ts is a 64-byte empty stub waiting for exactly this. Do a procedural juice pass (sprite tint, tween pops, particle rects, status pings — all on-brand for the no-assets pipeline). Bundle two honesty fixes that quietly erode trust: the paid-lie status upgrade, and the permanently-false flee reward.

Basis
directcombatFeedback.ts is a 64-byte stub (verified on disk); the enemy sprite is never flashed (flash() fires only on the player). cardUpgrade.ts:18 returns the status effect unchanged while line 21 appends + regardless — paying Gold to upgrade a pure-status card changes only the name. evaluateRoomThreatEscape returns grantBattleReward:false in all three branches, so the documented flee reward is unreachable.
externalThe 2024–25 pixel-art "comfort aesthetic" trend and the grounding's own read that the crowded genre is "under-explored on feel and accessibility" make polish a differentiation lever, not just hygiene.
Rationale

The highest juice-per-line in the project: the change improves the largest number of player-seconds (every attack of every fight), the home module already exists, and it requires no new art pipeline. The honesty fixes are small but matter — a core economy loop that charges a scarce resource and lies in the UI is the worst class of friction.

Downsides

Juice is easy to overdo (screen shake fatigue, illegible particle noise) — restraint and an accessibility toggle matter. The honesty fixes are quick wins but require deciding what a status upgrade should do (e.g. +1 stack/duration) and whether fleeing should pay at all (ties into I2's economy).

Combines with → I2 (decide the flee reward as part of the banking economy), I6 (a real status upgrade makes affliction builds viable).

Rejection Summary

Most of the 47 raw candidates were merged into the seven survivors above (e.g. the bluff, payoff-triangle, strong-enemy-scripting, and buy-down-info ideas all fused into I1). The table records the directions that were considered and cut or deferred, with why.

Idea Reason cut / deferred
Plan-vs-plan combat (commit a multi-round sequence) Compelling but a larger combat restructure; revisit as a brainstorm variant of I1 once the simpler payoff layer proves out.
Recruit the dead / third combatant Requires an N-actor rewrite of the hardcoded 1v1 resolveRound; high cost relative to the reads gains in I1/I5.
Transform-only rewards (no collection growth) Sweeping economy inversion; partially served by I2's reward-entropy fix and I6's curation — better as a brainstorm option than a default direction.
Full pacifist / stealth playstyle Needs the skip-balance modeling from I4 first; the concrete dead-flee-reward fix is folded into I7.
Fog-off routing (show every door's event) Routing-as-menu removes the dungeon's exploration tension; I2 delivers the same push-your-luck goal with less premise change.
One RunModifier abstraction (Daily + Ascension + challenges) Strong leverage but too broad to action as one direction; the Daily slice is served by I3, the rest is a later consolidation.
Clockwork / total-information puzzle fights The opposite of the double-blind core (I1); interesting as a separate combat texture, not this run's thrust.
Warden mode (build the dungeon, a ghost escapes) Large async-multiplayer surface; a genuine moonshot, far beyond current scope.
Cheating bosses (break engine rules) A memorable boss-design flourish; fold into boss authoring after I1/I4 land and more than 3 bosses exist.
Auto-expedition / idle descent Changes the game's active-play identity; park (the pre-run preview sub-idea is minor).

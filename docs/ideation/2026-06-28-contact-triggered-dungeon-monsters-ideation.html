Contact-Triggered Dungeon Monsters

A ranked idea set for making Escape's dungeon rooms playable before combat: monsters roam inside rooms, some chase, some ignore the player, and battles start only when contact happens.

Grounding Context

Current Contract

README.md describes encounter rooms as immediate fights, and DungeonScene.onRoomEntered() currently shows a red !, waits 450ms, then calls startBattle() for uncleared encounter or boss rooms.

Existing Engine Shape

The player already uses Arcade Physics, wall collision, body enable/reset during room transitions, and fixed proximity checks for potions, chests, rest spots, spikes, and the exit hatch.

Enemy Data Gap

Normal enemies have tier, HP, texture, and cards, but no dungeon movement or personality field. Bosses already have richer identity through specials, telegraphs, and distinct post-battle behavior.

Readability Risk

A repo-local Phaser layout learning says visible canvas behavior needs browser smoke, because state can be correct while on-screen feedback reads incorrectly. Roaming monsters will live or fail on timing, hitbox feel, and visual intent.

External grounding supports the same shape: visible field encounters often let enemies chase on alert and start combat on contact, but only work well when the player can read intent before the collision. That prior art reinforces contact, awareness, and markers as a combined direction rather than separate polish.

1. Contact-First Encounter Contract

Conf 92%Cx M

Replace room-entry auto-battle with a reusable encounter trigger. Entering an uncleared encounter room spawns the visible monster and arms contact detection; battle starts only when the player overlaps the monster body or tuned contact volume. The existing Battle scene, reward flow, and room-cleared cleanup remain the core handoff.

Axis
Trigger and room flow
Basis
direct: The repo currently starts combat from onRoomEntered() after a 450ms warning, while other dungeon interactions already use proximity or collision-style checks. Decoupling room entry from startBattle() is the necessary contract change.
Rationale
This is the smallest rule change that makes dungeon crawling spatial without replacing the card battle system. Every richer idea depends on this boundary.
Downsides
Requires careful transition safety, README/control text updates, and a clear decision on whether players can leave an encounter room without fighting.

2. Room Monster Actor Layer

Conf 86%Cx M

Introduce a dungeon-side monster actor that owns the sprite, physics body or contact volume, room bounds, behavior state, movement update, collision hookup, and cleanup. BuiltRoom would stop treating the monster as just enemy plus enemySprite, and instead keep a single actor object that can be updated, paused, and destroyed consistently.

Axis
Monster movement rules
Basis
direct: Enemies are currently plain images with idle tweens and no body, velocity, overlap, wall collision, or movement state, while the player already has those Arcade Physics patterns.
Rationale
This creates a foundation for contact, patrol, chase, pause/resume, and clearing behavior in one place instead of expanding DungeonScene.update() into a mix of unrelated rules.
Downsides
More structure than a quick distance check. It should stay small: one actor per current encounter, not a broad entity-component system.

3. Seeded Patrol And Wander Rules

Conf 84%Cx M

Use deterministic room-local movement modes: idle drift, horizontal or vertical lane patrol, small pause-and-step wander, center guard, and simple leash return after a chase. The rules should use the run RNG or room seed so daily and seeded runs produce repeatable pre-battle movement.

Axis
Monster movement rules
Basis
direct: Room generation is already seeded, rooms have fixed 15x11 grid geometry, trap placement already thinks in fair interior cells, and Battle already receives the run RNG.
Rationale
Rail-like and room-bounded movement gives life without full pathfinding. It is testable in pure helpers and predictable enough for balance.
Downsides
May look mechanical if every monster uses the same lane. Needs a few visually distinct modes and pause timing variation.

4. Behavior Profiles On Enemy Definitions

Conf 82%Cx S

Add lightweight dungeon behavior metadata to enemy definitions, with defaults by tier and optional per-enemy overrides. Example profile names: passive, wander, patrol, alertChase, guard, and bossPressure. Combat cards remain unchanged; the new profile affects only room behavior before contact.

Axis
Monster behavior and variety
Basis
direct: Normal enemies already have weak, medium, and strong tiers, but no movement or personality fields. Bosses already prove that enemy data can carry richer behavior through specials and telegraphs.
Rationale
This turns repeated textures and tier data into readable dungeon variety. Adding a new enemy can change traversal feel without writing custom scene code.
Downsides
Data names can become vague if not tied to exact behavior contracts. Keep profiles few and observable.

5. Alert, Chase, And Ignore Readability

Conf 80%Cx M

Move the existing red ! from room-entry countdown into monster-local intent. Passive monsters show no danger marker or a calm icon; suspicious monsters show a ?; chasing monsters show a red !, pulse, or tint. Contact then feels earned because the player saw the state change first.

Axis
Player readability and feedback
Basis
direct: The dungeon already has floating text, warnings, trap shake/tint, and a red pre-battle marker. external: Field encounter prior art uses visible enemies and intent markers to make chase/contact rules legible.
Rationale
The gameplay change is only fair if players can read which monsters are safe to skirt, which are watching, and which are actively closing distance.
Downsides
Markers can clutter the canvas if always visible. Prefer state-driven markers, short pulses, and browser smoke checks over permanent labels.

6. Contact Volume And Safe Entry Policy

Conf 78%Cx S

Define a named monster contact policy: a tuned contact radius or Arcade overlap, a short grace window after room transition, and safe spawn or patrol exclusion near entry cells. During the grace window the monster may animate or move, but cannot start battle until the player has regained control.

Axis
Trigger and room flow
Basis
direct: Existing transitions disable and reset the player body; existing pickups and traps use tuned distance/rectangle thresholds; trap rooms already use invulnerability timing.
Rationale
Contact-triggered battles can accidentally recreate auto-battle if a monster starts on top of the player or doorway. A visible safety policy protects the agency the feature is meant to add.
Downsides
Too much grace can make monsters feel harmless. Keep it short and explain behavior through placement rather than copy.

7. Boss Arena Contact Rule

Conf 72%Cx M

Treat boss rooms differently from normal encounter rooms. Normal monsters can be avoided until touched; bosses can guard the center, advance slowly, or arm combat when the player approaches an altar or threshold. The final room remains dramatic and hard to skip, while still using contact and visible telegraphing.

Axis
Trigger and room flow
Basis
direct: Bosses already differ in data, visuals, specials, victory aftermath, and room generation. reasoned: If every contact rule is identical, the boss can become accidentally optional or underwhelming.
Rationale
This keeps the normal-room agency gain while preserving the boss as a climax. It also gives a future place to connect dungeon movement telegraphs with boss battle telegraphs.
Downsides
Should not be part of the first thin slice unless the first implementation touches boss rooms. It can follow after normal encounter behavior proves out.

Rejection Summary

# Idea Reason Rejected
1 Remove the red bang countdown Duplicate of the stronger readability idea; kept as a detail inside intent markers.
2 Monster contact rules match pickup rules Useful implementation variant, but too tactical as a standalone direction.
3 Pre-battle bump feedback Important polish, folded into the contact contract and readability survivor.
4 Tabletop reaction roll Interesting variety generator, but better as a later variant of behavior profiles after baseline profiles exist.
5 Multi-monster rooms, single battle Scope overrun for the current request and increases room-state complexity before single-monster roaming is proven.
6 Encounter-plus rooms Good future expansion, but mixes room-generation redesign with the encounter-trigger change.
7 Touch advantage Expands into battle balance and reward context before the dungeon trigger has been validated.
8 Retreat door pressure Open design question, not a standalone idea; belongs in brainstorming for the contact contract.
9 Security-camera vision cones Potentially useful, but more precise and costly than alert radius plus intent markers needs for the first version.
10 Threat silhouette smoke test Essential validation practice, but not itself a product direction. Captured as a requirement on visible survivors.
